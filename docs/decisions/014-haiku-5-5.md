# 014 — Haiku 5.5 replaces Haiku 4.5, reads for the orchestrator, and takes a measured executor lane

Date: 2026-10-08
Status: accepted

## Context

Claude Haiku 5.5 (`claude-haiku-5-5`) shipped on 2026-10-07. Its system card
(144 pp.) and the measurements in
[`tests/eval/haiku-5-5-results-2026-10-08.md`](../../tests/eval/haiku-5-5-results-2026-10-08.md)
are the evidence for everything below.

- It is the first Haiku with effort levels, adaptive thinking and a 1M
  context window. It is priced by prompt length: $0.10 / $0.50 per million
  input / output tokens for a request of up to 100,000 prompt tokens, $0.50 /
  $2.50 above.
- On scoped coding at `medium` it is ahead of Sonnet 5.5 at `medium`
  (FrontierCode Main ≈41.6 against ≈36.5, chart read, p. 113) and behind
  Sonnet 5.5 at `high` (≈49.4); on complex terminal work it is far behind
  (Terminal-Bench 4.0 39.2 against 70.6, p. 115). Its behavioral-audit
  numbers are close to Sonnet 5.5's and far from Haiku 4.5's (false completion
  claims 1.89 against 1.60 and 4.85, p. 66), with more misread inputs than
  Sonnet 5.5 (1.88 against 1.44).
- The aliases had already moved under the skill: `haiku` resolved to
  `claude-haiku-5-5` and `sonnet` to `claude-sonnet-5-5`, while the alias
  table still said Haiku 4.5 and Sonnet 5.
- A child session of the native Claude runner started with 137,137 prompt
  tokens on a machine with MCP servers configured: with a built-in tool list
  that has no tool-search tool, every configured MCP tool schema is loaded
  inline. With `--strict-mcp-config` it starts with 10,047.
- For the same reason the drift judge's call carried 133,224 prompt tokens
  before the transcript. With Haiku 4.5's 200k window it failed on ordinary
  transcript tails; on three real tails of 175 KB to 854 KB it answered none
  of six calls.
- `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` is still Active; its retirement is promised no
  sooner than 2026-10-15.

## Decision

1. **Haiku 5.5 replaces Haiku 4.5 on its seats.** `claude-haiku-5-5` is a
   valid plan model and the first rung of the default ladder
   (`claude-haiku-5-5` → `claude-sonnet-5-5` → `claude-opus-5-5`), and the
   default model of the eval drivers. `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` stays valid
   so that approved plans still run, with a retired-route warning and the
   ladder it had. Its dossier entry stays for history.
2. **Child sessions start without MCP servers.** The native Claude runner and
   the drift judge pass `--strict-mcp-config`. A child's call to one of those
   tools was refused, because nothing can approve it in a headless session, so
   the child only paid for the definitions. One case changes: a tool that the
   user's or the project's settings already allow could be called by a runner
   child before and cannot now.
3. **The drift judge on Claude Code is `claude-haiku-5-5` at `low`.** The
   acceptance rule was written before the runs: no unavailable call, advice on
   every advice case, and on clean cases advice tolerated only on the one case
   documented as ambiguous plus at most one other run of eighteen. In the
   first measurement `medium`, the effort planned first, gave advice on two
   other clean runs, and the planned fallback, Sonnet 5.5, had an unavailable
   call. `low` was then measured under an order written after the `medium`
   result and before the alternative runs — the lowest list price among the
   judges that meet the rule — and met it. A repetition did not reproduce the
   difference: `medium` met the rule too. `low` stays as the judge measured
   through the shipped hook, on real tails and in noisy windows; it met the
   rule in four runs and gave advice on a clean case outside the ambiguous one
   in 0 of 60 runs, against 2 of 42 for `medium`. The prompt goes in on stdin,
   uncached, and the 45-second watchdog stops the judge process itself. The
   hook keeps its Claude dry-run line and keeps ignoring a judge override on a
   Claude host.
4. **Haiku 5.5 is the reader the orchestrator hands a file to.** Research
   Routing sends it pattern search, one closed lookup question, and the
   extraction of a fact from a large file, log or transcript handed over by
   path, at `medium`. Its report quotes the lines with line numbers, because it
   misreads inputs more often than the larger models. The orchestrator searches
   a large log or transcript itself first and hands it over only when the
   answer has to be read out of it: in a real session a reader took 21 s to
   return a line that one search finds. Wide enumeration stays on Sonnet 5.5
   and open research on Opus 5.5.
5. **Haiku 5.5 takes small closed tasks whose acceptance is fully
   mechanical**, at `medium`, with `"supervision": "mechanical"`. Adopted by
   rule L: on four such tasks, three waves per arm, it finished 12 of 12 on
   the first attempt, as Sonnet 5.5 did.
6. **Sonnet 5.5 may supervise a wave whose tasks are all Haiku 5.5 with empty
   ladders.** Adopted by rule S: the supervisor fixture passed 9 of 9 in each
   of three runs at `high` with the false-positive guard 15 of 15, and one
   real wave under its supervision ended with four sound verdicts.
7. **A spawn through an alias proves its model.** Agent-tool spawns use the
   aliases again; the agent states its exact model ID in the first line of its
   report, and a report from another model is discarded and the spawn repeated
   through Workflow `agent()` with the full ID.
8. **Telemetry prices a request by its own prompt size.** A price row may be
   an object with a prompt limit and two rate rows.

## Consequences

- A Haiku 5.5 task is cheap only while its requests stay under 100,000 prompt
  tokens. In the measured waves the largest executor request was 18,596
  tokens.
- With mechanical acceptance a green Haiku 5.5 task costs one model call.
  Under a model supervisor the supervisor is the expensive half: the same four
  tasks were reported at $0.017 with mechanical acceptance, $0.219 under
  Sonnet 5.5 and $0.464 under Opus 5.5.
- A drift check costs what its transcript tail weighs. One judge call was
  reported at $0.055 on a 175 KB tail (110,371 prompt tokens), $0.112 on a
  414 KB tail, $0.223 on an 854 KB tail and $0.250 on a 1.1 MB tail; with
  prompt caching left on, the first of them was reported at $0.110. All of them
  are above 100,000 prompt tokens and bill at the higher rate. The previous
  judge failed on the same tails and cost nothing.
- A tail larger than the model's window, 2.9 MB in the one measured case, is
  rejected and stays unjudged. Whether to cap the tail in bytes, so that a
  check stays under 100,000 prompt tokens, is open: a cap makes a check cheaper
  and takes evidence away from the judge.
- A hand-off to the reader costs little — $0.005 and $0.009 in the two
  measured sessions — and adds a sub-run of 21 s and 34 s. It pays only when it
  saves the orchestrator a long read.
- Aliases will move again. The alias table is dated; the model line in agent
  reports is what catches the next move.
- When Haiku 4.5 is retired, its ID leaves the accepted lists in one small
  change; nothing routes to it.

## Evidence and limits

The tables, commands and retained failures are in the results file. The limits
that matter for reading this decision:

- The executor-lane fixture did not separate the arms: both passed every run
  on the first attempt. It supports the lane as described in point 5 and says
  nothing about harder tasks; the card's Terminal-Bench gap is why the lane
  stops at mechanical acceptance.
- The drift cases are synthetic and few, and one held-out case was edited
  before the measurements, after both judges had reported its date as drift.
  `low` and `medium` are not separated by the rule: the two clean-case advice
  runs of `medium` did not recur in a repetition. The order that selected `low`
  was written after `medium` had missed the rule, with the pre-plan spike's
  `low` result already known. In real-size noisy windows the judge at `low`
  gave advice in 14 of 16 runs and missed planted drift in 2.
- The supervisor fixture shows that Sonnet 5.5 can judge four fixed cases, not
  a rate on real waves.
- The reader route is shown to work, not to be needed. In two real sessions
  the spawn went through the alias `haiku`, was billed to `claude-haiku-5-5`,
  and the report began with the model line and quoted the planted line with its
  number. Left to decide under the search-first rule, an Opus 5.5 seat settled
  both measured cases — a 9,000-line check log and a 340 KB executor
  transcript — with searches of its own and spawned no reader. On the skill's
  lookup path the workflow text is not loaded, so the rule does not apply
  there.
- One machine, one Claude Code version (2.1.293), one day.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Keep Haiku 4.5 as the drift judge, with `--strict-mcp-config`.** Two of 48
  calls unavailable under the 45-second watchdog, two advice runs naming the
  wrong task, a 200k window, and a retirement promise that runs out this
  month.
- **Sonnet 5.5 as the drift judge.** Silent on every clean run, but one of 48
  calls unavailable, at twenty times Haiku 5.5's price for a request under
  100,000 prompt tokens.
- **Haiku 5.5 at `medium` or `high` as the drift judge.** `medium` gave advice
  on two clean runs in the first measurement and on none in a repetition;
  nothing measured favours it over `low`, and a clean call was reported at the
  same cost at both. `high` caught less planted drift in noisy windows (10 of
  16 against 13 of 16 in the pre-plan spike).
- **A judge-override variable for the Claude host.** The hook deliberately
  ignores one there; another hook file given to the fixture driver measures
  another judge without a seam in production code.
- **Editing the two remaining fixture inconsistencies the `medium` judge
  cited.** A fixture is not softened to make a judge pass. Copies with those
  values aligned were measured outside the repository instead: no advice in 12
  runs at either effort, and none on the unchanged cases either.
- **Handing every large log to the reader at once.** Shipped first in this
  branch and withdrawn after a real session: the reader's sub-run took 21 s for
  a line one pattern search finds, and the orchestrator then printed more of
  the log into its own context than a session that searched the log itself.
- **Haiku 5.5 for wide enumeration, open research, a supervisor or reviewer
  seat, or an orchestrator seat.** Wide search collapses below `high` (WANDR
  12.9 at `medium`, p. 123); its self-preference as a grader is larger than
  Opus 5.5's (+0.23 of 9, p. 79); the card runs only a narrower alignment
  assessment on it (p. 55).
- **Removing `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` now.** Approved plans name it.
